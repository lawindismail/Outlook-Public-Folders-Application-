Public Folders - a small viewer for our Exchange public folders
=================================================================

The new Outlook doesn't show public folders. This app does.

HOW TO START
1. Copy "Public Folders.exe" anywhere, for example your Desktop or Documents.
   Nothing to install.
2. Double-click it. If Windows shows "Windows protected your PC", click
   "More info" and then "Run anyway". (The app isn't signed by a publisher.
   That's why Windows warns about it.)

HOW TO SIGN IN
- Easiest: choose "Classic Outlook" and click Connect. It uses the classic
  Outlook on your computer, so no login details are needed. Classic Outlook
  must be installed and set up with your work account; it can sit alongside
  the new Outlook. If Outlook asks whether to allow a program to access your
  mail, choose Allow.
- No classic Outlook? Choose "Exchange server", enter your work email and
  your Outlook password, and click Connect. The app finds the server by
  itself. "Where do I find these details?" in the app explains more.

WHAT IT DOES
Browse the public folder tree, read messages, posts, events and contacts,
filter a folder, star favourite folders, save attachments, and save
messages as files. It only reads; it doesn't change anything in the
public folders.

ADDING E-MAILS AND FILES
- Drag e-mails from Outlook (or files from File Explorer) onto a folder in
  the list on the left, then click Add.
- Or open a folder and click "Upload...", or right-click a folder and
  choose "Upload e-mails or files here...".
E-mail files go in as messages; other files go in as a post with the file
attached.

EXPORTING ONE MESSAGE OR FOLDER
Right-click a message: "Export..." saves the message file, and "Export with
attachments..." also saves its attachments as separate files.
Right-click a folder: "Export this folder..." saves it and its subfolders.

MOVING TO SHAREPOINT
Click "Export everything..." at the top. Every public folder, item and
attachment is saved to "Public Folders Project" on your Desktop, with the
same folder tree. You can then upload that folder to SharePoint.
_manifest.csv lists every item. Large exports can take hours; if one stops,
run it again and it carries on where it left off.

Your settings (not your password) are kept in
%APPDATA%\PublicFolderViewer\settings.json
